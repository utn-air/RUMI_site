In one sentence

RUMI brings robot-matched motion, live kinematic guidance, and contact force feedback into one handheld interface.

Robotiq 2F-85 kinematicsModular tactile fingersRobot-free to teleoperation
01 / The platform

Designed around the gripper it controls.

RUMI recreates the Robotiq 2F-85’s nonlinear finger motion and mounting geometry in a compact handheld form. The same interface can collect demonstrations away from the robot or command it remotely.

01
↗

Matched kinematics

A crossed scissor linkage maps thumb and index motion onto the 2F-85’s coupled fingers. An AS5600 encoder tracks aperture.

Embodiment-aware input
02
◉

Interchangeable fingertips

Swap compatible fingers for CoinFT force/torque sensing, DIGIT optical tactile sensing, or standard fingers.

One mount · multiple sensors
03
⌁

Contact you can feel

A servo-driven blocker engages after remote contact. Continued squeezing is measured and becomes a grasp-force command.

Aperture before contact · force after
02 / One interface, three modes

From hands-on practice to remote contact.

The physical input stays familiar as the contact setting changes. RUMI’s sensors and software adapt the feedback loop to the task.

A

Mixed reality · Collect in the workspace

Handle objects directly while a headset projects the target robot as a digital shadow. Fingertip sensors record local contact.

Local contact
B

Virtual reality · Practice in simulation

Control a simulated gripper. Contact engages the blocker, and squeeze force becomes a force-aware command.

Virtual contact
C

Teleoperation · Work through distance

Drive a real robot remotely with matched aperture control. When its gripper contacts an object, RUMI returns a mechanical cue and force input path.

Remote contact
03 / Robot-aware demonstrations

See what the robot can actually reach.

During robot-free collection, tracked RUMI poses drive an inverse-kinematics solution for a projected robot. Operators can spot workspace and joint-limit issues while recording.

05 / Takeaway

A consistent physical interface
for demonstration and control.

RUMI combines embodiment-matched motion, modular sensing, robot-aware visualization, and contact-triggered haptics to support tactile-enabled learning from demonstration.
